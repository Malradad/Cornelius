# Reading notes — Solo concept decks (2026)

Sources: 003 Al-Izdihar Villa Concept Design (24 pp, A3);
001 La Rustica Concept & Schematics 50% (26 pp, A3).
Status: maker confirms both are entirely his. Highest-weight evidence in the codex.

## His deck system (identical in both)
1. Cover: big project number (001, 003) + "CONCEPT DESIGN".
2. Epigraph page with a wink: "THE PROJECT MAY CONTAIN ETHEREAL IMAGERY" /
   "THE PROJECT MAY CONTAIN PIZZA", plus a look-and-feel disclaimer.
3. Introduction · Premise · Content.
4. Roman-numeral chapters: Context (existing house / the brand) → Proposal
   abstract (a principle set) → User or Origin → (Urban motifs) → Design
   narrative (Aesthetics, Materials, Lighting, Furniture, Sketches) → Summary → Glossary.
5. A principle set as the spine: SERENE · CURATORIAL · ALLUSIVE (villa);
   RAW · WARM · TACTILE · INTIMATE, then LAYERED · CONTRASTED · NOVELTY (La Rustica).
6. A glossary of 70–90 terms that defines his own vocabulary.
7. Copyright page. "DESIGN BY MESHAL ALRADADI" on every page.

## Thinking
- **Between continuity and transformation.** Both decks use the phrase. The
  inherited condition (Baroque villa, existing brand, Naples) is treated as a
  productive constraint, not a blank slate.
- **The inherited object as artefact.** The original Spanish door is isolated
  and re-presented against quiet timber: "an inherited object with its own
  presence." This is the Imaginary Variance graft, inverted, at domestic scale.
- **Allusion over quotation.** "A curve may recall a classical arch without
  becoming one."
- **The threshold is central again.** La Rustica has a whole chapter on
  thresholds ("a threshold can reveal, conceal, invite, or stage"); the villa
  has glass block, reeded glass, and "redefined thresholds". Strong confirmation
  of the boundary obsession.
- **Pairs again:** rustic/pristine, matte/polished, opaque/translucent,
  warm/cool, old/new, domestic/exhibitive.
- **The user as a character:** "ESCAPISTS: IMAGINING UTOPIAN WORLDS".
- **Research first:** origin, history, urban motifs, precedents, then design.

## Visual language (interiors)
- **Collage sketches:** an infinite white perspective grid floor; cut-out
  furniture and materials; classical statues standing on the grid; line-drawn
  diners over a photo collage. A metaphysical, de Chirico-like stage. It is
  the grey-ghost/colored-graft method from the thesis, now inside a room.
- **Surreal epigraph images:** a cypress on a rock over a house; an arch in the
  desert framing a tree and clouds.
- **Palette rule:** a warm, dark base plus ONE cool counterpoint.
  Villa: dark timber, plaster, limestone + green marble. La Rustica: brick,
  wood + blue-grey plaster.
- **Light:** always indirect, diffused, a glow; source hidden; "cinematic".
  Explicitly against exposed downlights.
- **Furniture:** collected, not decorated. Italian mid-century and modernism,
  sculptural objects, vintage pieces given room to be read. Arches recur.
- **Layout:** Helvetica-style grotesque, bold caps chapter titles plus Roman
  numerals, big white space, image clusters, small running footer.

## Voice
- Far closer to his own voice than EB2: real ideas (curatorial, allusive,
  inherited object, threshold as stage). Brochure words mostly gone
  ("immersive" 3–4 times vs. 11 in EB2).
- New tics replace them. La Rustica: "warmth" ×35, "atmosphere" ×32, "layered" ×31,
  "resonant/resonance" ×11. Villa: "atmosphere" ×19, "warmth" ×14, "layered" ×12.
- Every chapter re-explains the principle set; the summary repeats the whole
  deck. Text is long for a client deck.
- The epigraph jokes are the most "him" lines in either deck. Wit is underused.
